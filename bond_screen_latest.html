How to read this. A bond is flagged only when its discount survives a rate adjustment. Spread is measured against the Treasury curve as of the mark date, then compared to what its own market was paying that day — the ICE BofA high-yield or investment-grade index for corporates, the median spread of same-maturity municipals for munis. Flags fire on excess spread (≥150bp stressed, ≥700bp distressed), never on dollar price alone: a zero-coupon muni at 78 is arithmetic, not distress. Only bonds at or below 95 are screened, because an issuer will not call a bond trading below par — so in that regime yield-to-maturity equals yield-to-worst and no call schedule is needed. This is a rate-adjusted discount screen, not an option-adjusted spread.
What this does not measure. Universe = bonds these funds still held and publicly reported at each date. Funds can sell deteriorating credits before a reporting date and may exit defaulted bonds, so this is a lower bound on healthcare credit stress and can lag or understate deterioration. Marks are fund fair-value marks published roughly 60 days in arrears, not trades. Denominators are printed for every figure.
