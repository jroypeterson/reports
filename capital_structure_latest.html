Company-first, not instrument-first. Every bond and loan one borrower has outstanding, in one table, ordered by maturity — the view that sits next to an equity position. Bonds are quoted on yield to maturity and spread; loans on yield-at-reset and discount margin, because a fixed-rate yield on a floating coupon would be invented. The Mark column is the fund's own fair-value level: L3 means it could not observe a price and modelled one, which qualifies every other number on that row. A blank yield means floating-rate far more often than it means missing — those notes reset off an index, so a fixed-rate yield would be invented; the reason is printed in the cell.
This is not an issue-size ladder. The panel knows what these funds hold, not what was issued — so the bars count instruments, not dollars outstanding, and a maturity nobody in this fund set owns does not appear at all. Read it as "what is visible", not "what exists".
