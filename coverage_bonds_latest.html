What you are looking at. Every bond held by the harvested bond funds whose issuer maps to a company in your coverage universe — 256 Core=Y names plus 57 large-cap Biopharma names admitted by the same screen the sector chart pack uses (market cap ≥ $10B; Large Pharma, and Biotech only where gross margin is positive, which excludes pre-revenue R&D companies whose credit has no product economics behind it).

Where the prices come from. Bond funds must publish their holdings quarterly, and each holding carries a market value and a par balance — so value ÷ par is a fair-value mark on every bond a fund owns. That is the entire price source. It is not a trade print, and it runs roughly 60 days behind, so this tells you where a bond is, not where you could deal.

Read yield, not price. Dollar price is mostly a statement about coupon and maturity, not credit: a 2% coupon 2050 bond sits in the 70s on rates alone, while a distressed short bond can sit at 99. Everything here is therefore ranked and cut on YTM. Since no call schedules are available from any free source, yield-to-worst cannot be computed — below par the two are identical anyway, and above par YTM is the optimistic one. Loans are different: they pay a floating coupon, so their yield column is the yield at the current reset and the spread column is a discount margin over SOFR — a fixed-rate YTM on a floater would be a fabricated number.

What is missing, and why. A company appears only if one of the harvested funds owns its debt. Absence means no fund held it — not that no bond exists. Several subsectors are legitimately empty because their companies borrow via bank term loans rather than bonds (Healthcare IT, Post-Acute and Skilled Nursing especially); those rows are listed below with their names so the gap is explicit rather than invisible.
