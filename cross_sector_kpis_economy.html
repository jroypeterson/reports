Fiscal-period reconciliation

86 of 123 measured cells are calendar-aligned; 37 are not. Period basis across live rows — 16 measured (the filing states start and end dates), 0 point-in-time snapshots at a month end, and 14 assumed calendar (marked ~: earnings_kpi stores a fiscal quarter label with no dates, and managed-care filers are calendar filers, but this sheet says it is assuming that rather than implying it checked). On those rows the period-length comparability check could not run either — a 53rd week or a ragged quarter passes through as growth there, where a dated row would have been withheld. That is the cost of the missing dates, and it is larger than the placement question it is usually described as.

Every cell is placed by the nearest calendar quarter end to its reporting period's end, so a fiscal quarter running Nov–Jan lands in calendar Q4, not Q1. (This paragraph said "mid-point" until 2026-08-08; the code has always snapped on the end. The two agree for a 13-week quarter, which is why it went unnoticed — but a sheet whose whole claim is honesty about method cannot misdescribe its own.) Fiscal year-end offset is not the same thing as period-boundary offset: McKesson (March year-end), Cencora (September) and Cardinal Health (June) all carry fiscal labels that differ from the calendar while their period dates line up exactly.
